The problem

Local waste caps out at ~18 BF. Strength lives overseas.

Recycled Indian OCC is short-fibre and over-recycled. To climb the BF ladder — and to get edge-crush strength (RCT), not just burst — mills must blend in imported long fibre made from virgin wood pulp, which India can't produce (no fresh wood-pulp capacity).

The technical crux

Starch buys burst. It can't buy compression.

This is why a cheap additive alone never fully replaced imported fibre — and why the real answer must rebuild the fibre network, not just the bonding.

Starch / basic bonding

BF (burst)
high
RCT (compression)
low

Boosts inter-fibre bonding → burst & tensile. Leaves edge-crush / stacking strength short.

Long imported fibre

BF (burst)
high
RCT (compression)
high

Rebuilds the load-bearing fibre network → the RCT that boxes need to stack. That's what the import actually buys.

The insight — Speaker 2's framing

Don't replace the tonnage. Engineer a hybrid.

No single cheap raw material fully replaces imported long fibre at half the cost with full RCT and no machine change. The deliverable win is a hybrid furnish — cheap Indian base, plus added strength inputs dosed into the existing line.

Business model

How it makes money — and stays defensible.

Value proposition

Cut imported-fibre dependence 30–60% at a net cost saving — same paper, no machine change, stocked locally and priced daily, with zero 2-month shipment lock-in.

Customer segments

  • Beachhead: 50 already-associated mills
  • Core: small/mid recycled-kraft mills — Gujarat (Morbi, Vapi, Ahmedabad)
  • Scale: 500+ mills pan-India

Revenue streams

  • Gain-share on the Strength Kit (₹/tonne of paper)
  • Trading margin on domestic long fibre
  • Recurring re-supply + recipe service

Pricing — gain-share

  • Split the saving: mill keeps ~half, you keep ~half
  • Priced per tonne improved, not per kg of additive
  • Daily index pricing — no booking risk

Key activities

  • Blend & stock the kit; source domestic fibre
  • Run mill trials, tune recipes per grade
  • Hold local inventory for daily delivery

Key partners

  • Chemical makers (Solenis/BASF, Kemira, Ecolab, Buckman)
  • Enzyme suppliers
  • Box plants & converters (trim supply)

Cost structure

  • Chemistry + enzyme COGS
  • Warehouse, inventory & working capital
  • Small technical-service team; patent/IP

Who's already working on it

The gap you walk into

Global majors — Solenis/BASF, Kemira, Ecolab, Buckman — already sell dry-strength "reduce-your-fibre" programs, but chase large integrated mills. Indian R&D (ICAR-CIRCOT on MFC, NITI Aayog on bamboo) is early-stage.

  • No one owns the simple, warehoused, per-tonne-priced kit for small kraft mills
  • Those mills buy imported fibre by habit, not because chemistry was productised for them
  • That bottom-of-market gap is the whole opportunity
