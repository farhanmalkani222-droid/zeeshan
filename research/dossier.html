1 · Executive summary

Indian mills make 16–18 BF kraft easily from local recycled waste. For 22 BF and above — and for RCT (edge-crush strength), not just burst — they must blend in imported US/EU waste paper that carries long virgin fibre India cannot produce. That import is the pain: volatile ocean freight, a ~2-month price lock-in, and ~₹25–30/kg landed cost.

The honest finding. No single cheap Indian material is half the price of US waste on a like-for-like tonne. The real ~50% saving comes from an all-India blend whose cost to hit the same BF + RCT is about half: a cheap domestic base + a small share of domestic long fibre (jute waste, kraft trim, bamboo) + strength chemistry that multiplies each fibre's strength — dropped in where the import went, no machine change.

2 · The problem, decoded

PROBLEM

Recycled Indian OCC is short-fibre and over-recycled, so it caps out around 18 BF. Climbing the BF ladder — and getting compression strength — needs long virgin fibre, imported because India has no fresh wood-pulp capacity (forest limits).

GradeHow it is made todayImported-fibre dependence
16–18 BF100% Indian OCC + starchNone
20–24 BFIndian OCC + 30–50% importedRising
28–35 BFImport-heavy blendHigh
40–45 BFPremium US DLK/DSOCCLocked to imports

The technical crux: starch buys burst, not compression

PropertyWhat drives itStarch / bondingLong imported fibre
BF (Burst Factor)Inter-fibre bondingHigh ✓High ✓
RCT (Ring Crush)Fibre network / stiffnessLow ✗High ✓

The import buys two things at once — long fibre for RCT and bonding surface for BF. Starch replaces only the second. Any real substitute must rebuild the compression-bearing fibre network, which is the hard, valuable part.

The four pains: volatile ocean freight (Red Sea) · ~2-month shipment lead with price lock-in · US$150–250/t fibre · ~10% furnish yield loss. The durable pain is lead-time + working capital + margin squeeze in an over-supplied market — not freight alone.

Where the 50% actually comes from

Not one fibre cheaper than US waste (virgin bamboo pulp ≈ ₹58/kg is dearer). The saving is on the blended cost of hitting the grade: replace expensive imported waste with a cheap base + a small long-fibre share + chemistry that lets less long fibre do the work.

LayerIndia sourceRoleIndicative cost
Base 60–70%Domestic OCC + bagasse/wheat strawBulk furnish₹10–15/kg
Long fibre 15–25%Jute waste / gunny · domestic kraft trim · bamboo (premium)Delivers RCT₹8–22/kg
MultiplierGPAM + PVAm + starch + enzymeMore strength per fibre+₹1–2.5/kg paper
= Blend—Same BF/RCT, India-only₹12–18/kg (~40–50% less)

Why it genuinely works, not just a cost trick

  • Jute / recovered gunny — long, strong bast fibre; documented as able to replace long-fibre bamboo and imported softwood; can repulp in the mill's existing hydrapulper (drop-in, no new plant — validate smell/cleaning).
  • Bamboo — 2–3 mm fibre ≈ softwood → full RCT; and the legal unlock: since the 2017 Forest Act amendment, bamboo on non-forest land is not a "tree" (no permit, freely traded, government-promoted). Buy market pulp or toll-pulp; don't build a mill.
  • Domestic kraft trim / DLK — imported-grade long fibre, generated in India, no sea freight.
  • Chemistry + enzyme — "raw materials you add" at the existing wet-end/refiner.

6 · Business model

MODEL
Value proposition: Cut imported-fibre dependence 30–60% at a net cost saving — same paper, no machine change, stocked locally and priced daily, zero 2-month shipment lock-in.

Revenue streams

  • Gain-share on the strength kit (₹/tonne paper)
  • Trading margin on domestic long fibre
  • Recurring re-supply + recipe service

Pricing — gain-share

  • Split the saving: mill keeps ~half, you keep ~half
  • Priced per tonne improved, not per kg additive
  • Daily index pricing — no booking risk

Customer segments

  • Beachhead: 50 associated mills
  • Core: small/mid kraft mills — Gujarat (Morbi/Vapi/Ahmedabad)
  • Scale: 500+ pan-India

Key partners

  • Chemical makers (Solenis/BASF, Kemira, Ecolab, Buckman)
  • Enzyme (Advanced Enzymes, Novonesis)
  • Box plants & jute mills (fibre)

9 · Risks, caveats & next steps

DECISION

Risks & honest caveats

  • No blind guarantee — results depend on the mill's furnish, water and machine; expect 1–2 dose iterations.
  • Highest grades (35–45 BF) lean more on bamboo → cost gap narrows; prove the 18–28 BF sweet spot first.
  • Smell & drainage are the real watch-items with jute/gunny & agro.
  • Chemistry is partly commoditised — the moat is IP + local stock + service + the small-mill channel, not the molecules.
  • Credible incumbents (Yash Pakka, Ruchira, KR, bamboo plants) could move down-market; speed + patent matter.
  • All ₹ figures indicative — validate by quotation and on a live mill before committing capital.

Immediate next steps

  • Pick one associated mill; capture its current BF, RCT, furnish %, imported-fibre cost, starch dose.
  • Get trial samples: chemistry/enzyme kits + a few hundred kg of each Indian fibre.
  • Run the 4–6 week trial (section 8); measure ₹/tonne saved & % imports displaced.
  • If net-positive: lock the formulation, file IP, set up the Gujarat warehouse, start gain-share sales.
  • In parallel, scout 5–10 mills + suppliers to confirm the model is still open.
