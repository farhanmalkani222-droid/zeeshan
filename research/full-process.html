Full Process, Sourcing & Test Recipe

India-made kraft fibre to match the US import — Gujarat-local sources, exact proportions to test, and the pass/fail tests. Final direction: recovered kraft sacks + cotton + chemistry (bamboo dropped: NE-grown, needs pulping).

Prepared 2 Oct 2026 · Companion to the research dossier & mill-trial protocol · Prices indicative (India 2025) — confirm by quotation. Proportions are starting points to validate on trial.

CORE IDEA   Used kraft sacks (cement/chemical/food) are made from virgin long-fibre sack kraft — the same fibre class as imported DLK/DSOCC, but collected in Gujarat. Grade it to a spec, blend with a little cotton waste + a cheap OCC/bagasse base, add strength chemistry/enzyme, and the mill drops it into its existing pulper where the US waste went — no machine change.

1 · The process, end to end

1
Collect (Gujarat-local). Used cement/chemical/food kraft sacks, box-plant trim/DLK, cotton mill waste/linter, domestic OCC, bagasse. All sourced within the state → near-zero freight, daily price, no shipment lock.
2
Sort & grade to a written spec. Remove plastic liners, strings, tape; separate by fibre quality & cleanliness; control moisture. Define grades (e.g. "India-DLK Grade A / B") the way imported bales carry grades.
3
QC each incoming lot. Moisture, visible contamination, and a quick handsheet for BF/RCT contribution. Reject or down-grade off-spec lots. This grading + QC is the value-add that makes it "import level."
4
Blend to recipe (section 3). Bale the graded blend as a single stock-keeping product per target BF grade.
5
At the mill — no machine change. Add the blend to the existing hydrapulper where imported waste went; dose starch/GPAM/PVAm at the wet-end and enzyme at the refiner.
6
Run & sample. Make paper for a controlled window; pull reel samples.
7
Test vs control (section 4): BF, RCT, Cobb, GSM, colour, smell, runnability. Cost it per tonne.
8
Standardise & repeat. Lock the winning recipe + grade spec; QC every future lot to it so quality stays at import level forever.

Decision: the cheapest Mix that PASSES all of BF, RCT, colour, smell and runnability = your product. Standardise its recipe + the grade spec; QC every future lot to hold import-level quality.

Honest notes: Smell and drainage are the real watch-items — keep them as hard pass/fail lines. 18–32 BF is where a graded kraft-sack blend matches import level; 40–45 BF may still need the highest virgin fibre (trial shows how far).
