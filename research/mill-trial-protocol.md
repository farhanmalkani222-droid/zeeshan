# Mill Trial Protocol — India Long-Fibre Blend vs US Imported Fibre

**Goal:** Prove on one real mill that an **all-India furnish** matches the US imported-fibre grade on **BF, RCT, colour and smell**, runs on the **same machine**, and costs **~40–50% less per tonne of furnish** — before scaling to other mills.

**Status of numbers:** Prices and dosages below are **indicative market figures (India, 2025)** gathered from public trade listings — treat them as *starting points to confirm by quotation*, not fixed quotes. Test targets and methods are industry-standard. Sources at the end.

---

## 0. One-page quick version

1. **Pick one grade** to copy exactly — e.g. the mill's current **28 BF** testliner that uses imported fibre.
2. **Run the CONTROL** (their present imported recipe) and record BF, RCT, GSM, Cobb, colour, smell, machine speed. This is the benchmark to beat/match.
3. **Run 3 TEST blends** (below) built only from Indian materials + strength chemistry/enzyme.
4. **Measure the same properties** on each, by the standard test methods (section 4).
5. **Pass = ** BF within **±1 point**, RCT **≥ 95%** of control, colour visually matched (ΔE small), **smell neutral** on a 5-person panel, machine speed **≥ 95%** of control, and furnish cost **≤ 60%** of the imported recipe.
6. **Cost it** on the sheet in section 6. If a blend passes and saves, you have your product and your proof.

**Timeline:** ~4–6 weeks (1 week sourcing samples, 1 week lab handsheets, 2–3 weeks machine trials + retest, closeout).

---

## 1. The recipes to run

> %s are **% of total fibre furnish by oven-dry weight**. Chemistry doses are **on dry fibre**. Start here, then let the winning blend be fine-tuned.

| | **CONTROL** (benchmark) | **T1 — drop-in, no pulping** | **T2 — + bamboo premium** | **T3 — optimise winner** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Domestic OCC | per current | 70% | 60% | tune |
| Imported fibre | per current (e.g. 40%) | **0%** | **0%** | **0%** |
| Bagasse (cheap filler) | – | – | 10% | tune |
| **Jute waste / old gunny** (long-fibre) | – | 15% | 10% | tune |
| **Domestic kraft trim / DLK** | – | 15% | 10% | tune |
| **Bamboo unbleached pulp** (premium RCT) | – | – | 10% | tune |
| Cationic starch | current | 1.0% | 1.0% | tune |
| GPAM | – | 0.2% | 0.2% | tune |
| Dry-strength resin / PVAm | – | 0.3% | 0.3% | tune |
| Refiner enzyme (cellulase blend) | – | 0.3 kg/t | 0.3 kg/t | tune |

**Logic:** T1 tests whether the **cheapest, no-new-plant** route (jute/gunny + domestic trim + chemistry) already matches the benchmark. T2 adds a little **bamboo pulp** for the hardest RCT. T3 locks the cheapest blend that passes.

**Dose ranges to explore** (if first pass misses): cationic starch 0.5–1.5%, GPAM 0.1–0.3%, dry-strength resin/PVAm 0.2–0.5%, enzyme 0.1–0.5 kg/t, long-fibre share 10 → 25%.

## 3. Equipment (all standard in/near any kraft mill QC lab)

Hydrapulper (mill's own) · lab refiner/valley beater · **handsheet former** (ISO 5269) · **Mullen burst tester** · **ring-crush tester** · GSM balance · **Cobb tester** · thickness gauge · moisture meter/oven · (optional) **colourimeter/spectrophotometer** for ΔE; else a daylight light-box for visual match.

---

## 4. Test methods & targets (this is the "accurate data" that decides it)

| Property | Standard method | Instrument | **Pass target vs control** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GSM (grammage)** | ISO 536 | Balance + area | Match target grade ±2% |
| **BF (Burst Factor)** | Burst strength: **IS 1060 / ISO 2758 / TAPPI T403**; BF = burst (gf/cm²) ÷ GSM | Mullen burst tester | **Within ±1 BF point** of control (e.g. 28 → ≥27) |
| **RCT (Ring Crush)** | **ISO 12192 / TAPPI T822** (rigid support) | Ring-crush tester | **≥ 95%** of control RCT (N or kgf) |
| **CMT (if fluting)** | ISO 7263 / TAPPI T809 | Crush tester | ≥ 95% of control |
| **Cobb (water absorption)** | ISO 535 | Cobb tester | Within ±10% of control |
| **Colour / shade** | ΔE vs control, or daylight light-box visual | Spectrophotometer / light-box | ΔE small; **visually indistinguishable** |
| **Smell** | 5-person blind sniff panel, fresh + after 24 h in a closed bag | — | **Neutral / no off-odour** vs control |
| **Moisture** | ISO 287 | Oven / meter | Within spec |
| **Machine runnability** | Drainage (°SR/CSF), wire/press behaviour, speed | Mill DCS | **Speed ≥ 95%** of control; no new breaks |

**Why these four matter to the customer:** BF + RCT = will the box hold and stack; Colour + Smell = will the buyer accept it as the same product. All four must pass, not just strength.

---

## 5. Procedure (step by step)

**Week 1 — Source & baseline.** Order trial samples of every material (section 2). Run the **CONTROL** on the machine (or lab handsheets first) and fully characterise it — this is your benchmark row.

**Week 2 — Lab handsheets.** Make handsheets of **T1, T2** at the section-1 doses. Measure all section-4 properties. Cheap, fast screening before using machine time. Adjust doses if a target is missed.

**Weeks 3–4 — Machine trials.** Run the best 1–2 blends on the paper machine for a controlled window (e.g. 2–4 hours each). Pull reel samples, retest everything incl. **runnability and smell**. Tune to **T3**.

**Weeks 5–6 — Confirm & cost.** Repeat the winning T3 to confirm repeatability. Fill the cost sheet (section 6). Write the one-page result: pass/fail per property + ₹/tonne saved.

## 7. Honest caveats
- **No blind guarantee:** this protocol is how you *prove* it; results depend on the mill's furnish, water and machine. Expect 1–2 dose iterations.
- **Highest grades (35–45 BF)** will lean more on bamboo pulp → cost gap narrows there; prove the mid grades (18–28 BF) first, where the win is biggest and easiest.
- **Smell and drainage** are the two real watch-items with jute/gunny and agro — that's exactly why they're explicit pass/fail lines above.
- **Confirm every price by quotation.** The figures here are public-listing indicative ranges for planning, not firm quotes.
